## Method

- Each question was written from the trial's registered conditions, interventions and design, phrased the way a clinician or coordinator would ask rather than copying the registry title. The gold answer is one specific NCT ID; a different but clinically reasonable trial ranked first still counts as a miss.
- Questions run through `query_documents` — the same hybrid (pgvector cosine + Postgres full-text, fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion) query `semantic_search` serves to Claude.
- Scored against the **full** local corpus, not the bundled demo slice.

## What this does not show

- **The labeller wrote the questions knowing the corpus.** That biases results upward versus questions written by someone who has never seen it.
- **20 questions is a spot-check, not a benchmark.** Treat the percentages as indicative; the confidence interval on 20 items is wide.
- **One gold answer per question.** Oncology registries contain many near-duplicate trials, so a 'miss' is often a defensible alternative rather than a bad result — read the misses above rather than the headline number.
- **Clinical corpus only.** The openFDA and PubMed corpora are not scored here.
- Re-run with `uv run python scripts/eval_retrieval.py` after any retrieval change; results move with the corpus and the embedding model.

## Error analysis — 2026-09-21 run

**The one outright miss is a vocabulary problem, not a ranking problem.** q02 asks for
"trastuzumab deruxtecan versus T-DM1"; the labelled trial (`NCT03529110`, DESTINY-Breast03)
is registered under the drug's *development code* — "DS-8201a Versus T-DM1 …" — and never
says "trastuzumab deruxtecan" in the text that gets embedded. It ranks **28th**. Its sibling
DESTINY-Breast05, which spells the generic name out in the title, ranks 1st for the same
question. Registry titles mix development codes (DS-8201a, MK-3475, AMG 510) with generic
names, and the embedding only sees the words that are there.

**The fix, and why it isn't applied yet.** The clinical connector embeds `brief_title` +
`brief_summary` (`servers/clinical/server.toml`), while the drug synonyms sit in the
`interventions` array of the structured payload — `NCT03529110` lists "Trastuzumab deruxtecan
(T-DXd)" there. Adding `interventions` to `embed_fields` should close most of this gap.
It means re-embedding the whole clinical corpus, so it is a deliberate next step rather than
a hotfix, and this report is the evidence for making it. Tracked in [`TODO.md`](../TODO.md).

**The near-misses are mostly the corpus being honest.** q04, q05, q07, q08 and q18 each
returned a clinically reasonable trial above the labelled one — a newer durvalumab biliary
study, a different EGFR combination, an investigator-initiated ipilimumab/nivolumab melanoma
study. Scored strictly as misses here; a reader should treat hit@3 (80%) and hit@10 (95%) as
the more meaningful numbers for "did the right study surface at all".
